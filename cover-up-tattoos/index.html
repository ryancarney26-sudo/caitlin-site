Cover-up tattoos in Preston

A cover-up tattoo hides an old tattoo inside a new one you actually want. I do cover-ups and reworks at True Colours in Preston: faded names, blown-out lines, holiday tattoos, and pieces that just weren't done well.

How I work

[Caitlin, two or three lines: what a good cover-up needs, and a cover-up you were proud of.]

Send a daylight photo first. The old tattoo's size, darkness and colour decide what can go over it. A clear photo in daylight tells me most of what I need before you come in.

Dark covers light. A cover-up has to be bigger and usually darker than what's under it. Black and grey and deep colours work best. A pale, delicate design won't hide an old black line, however much we'd like it to.

Design around it, not just over it. The best cover-ups use the old shape: a name becomes the stem of a flower, a blurred blob becomes the shadow in a bigger piece. That's why I ask for the photo before I draw anything.

Sometimes a rework, not a cover. An old tattoo can often be refreshed instead: lines sharpened, shading added, a weak design strengthened. It's cheaper, and you keep what you liked.

Sometimes laser first. Very dark or large old tattoos may need one or two laser sessions to fade them, which opens up far more design choices. True Colours offers laser removal, so it can all happen under one roof.

Questions about cover-ups

How much does a cover-up cost?

Cover-ups are priced by the session, from [£___], because they're bigger and take longer than a fresh tattoo of the same design. I'll quote from your photo before you pay anything.

Can every tattoo be covered?

Nearly all of them, with the right design and size. Some need laser fading first. Very dark, large or raised tattoos are the hard ones. Send me a photo and I'll tell you straight.

How long after laser can I have the cover-up?

Usually at least [eight weeks] after the last laser session, once the skin has fully settled. Rushing it makes the new tattoo heal badly.
